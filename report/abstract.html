We port the authors' released code for DeepQCD [1] function by function and use nothing else: the notebook's network, training loop, transient data generator, window-limited CUSUM and false-alarm/miss counting, and the IoT script's way of turning recordings into streams. Part I reruns the paper's Section 5 at the published settings and at others, and the authors' saved notebook outputs fall on our curves. In the Bayesian setting DeepQCD comes within 6% of the optimal Shiryaev procedure in six of seven settings. Its minimax advantage over CUSUM appears only because delay is measured with the change at τ = 1; with the change at τ = 200 it vanishes or reverses. Its transient advantage holds at the paper's setting (PD 0.87 vs 0.86 at PFA 0.05), but not for weaker or shorter changes. Part II runs the transient experiment of Section 5.3, unchanged, on six real datasets chosen by four stated conditions. With the authors' stream construction and the paper's τ₁ = 1000, K = 25, DeepQCD (mean of three seeds) does not beat the window-limited CUSUM on HAI, SMD, SKAB or Occupancy, and neither method detects Parkinsonian freezing. DeepQCD leads only on Occupancy without the light sensor, and that lead disappears once the splice between recordings is removed. Splicing enlarges the step at the change point up to twentyfold, so unspliced results are reported alongside. Four questions for the next round close the report.
