Reproduction

Trained exactly as in the notebooks, DeepQCD reproduces every Section 5 figure, and the authors' saved numbers fall on our curves (open circles). Its Bayesian delay at PFA = 0.01 is within 6% of the optimal Shiryaev procedure, or its modified AR version, in six of the seven settings tried, and 16% above it for the weak AR drift μ₁ = 0.5 (Figs. 5 and 7). This confirms the paper's main claim.

The minimax advantage of Figs. 6 and 8 is real only for the delay measured with the change at τ = 1. With the change at τ = 200, DeepQCD is never more than 1% faster than CUSUM. It is 2–15% slower at FAP = 1,000, about tied at FAP = 10,000, and far slower at FAP = 100, where its early false alarms dominate. This is what the Lorden-optimality of CUSUM requires. At τ = 1 the network benefits from a statistic that starts high, and it pays for that with false alarms early in each stream; the paper's protocol counts the first and not the second.

The transient result (Fig. 9) reproduces at the paper's setting. However, the window-limited CUSUM leads for a weaker change (μ = 0.5: PD 0.17 vs 0.12 at PFA 0.05) and for shorter ones (K = 10), because it is told the duration and the network is not.

One practical note: the minimax notebook as released generates the benchmark data with one noise draw copied to all seven coordinates (cells 21, 23, 28 and 30). The authors' saved outputs match the correct generator, so the published figure is unaffected, but anyone re-running the notebook gets a far worse CUSUM (§2, checkbox). A one-line fix in the release would prevent that.

  • No real dataset reproduces the paper's simulated edge under its own protocol. On HAI and SKAB the two methods are within the seed-to-seed spread of DeepQCD (0.34–0.54 on HAI). On SMD the CUSUM leads clearly: a network trained on 14 server machines does not transfer to 14 unseen ones. Restricted to z, the same network reaches 0.21, close to the CUSUM, so here the extra sensors hurt rather than help.
  • Daphnet is beyond both at K = 25. A freeze changes the tremor frequency, not the mean, and 25 samples (0.8 s) are too few to tell it from walking at PFA ≤ 0.05.
  • Occupancy with the light sensor is trivial, because the lights switch on at arrival. Without the light sensor, DeepQCD leads in spliced streams (0.47 vs 0.20), but both methods miss all six real arrivals when the stream is not spliced.

Splicing. Table 6 explains the Occupancy result. At a real arrival the sensors move by 0.12 standardized units in the first minutes; at a spliced one, by 2.79. In the blocks construction the detectors are rewarded for noticing that two recordings were joined, and the network, which was also trained on spliced streams, learns that cue. HAI shows a related effect: its normal operation moves between operating points, so two normal blocks differ more (9.8) than a real attack onset changes the data (3.4). The native results come from few events (19 to 57), so their curves move in steps of 1/n. Still, they are the better guide to performance in deployment; the blocks results are the ones comparable with the authors' evaluation.

Limits. There is one network (the Section 5 LSTM) and one benchmark model (Gaussian, along z). That model fits poorly where test events differ from training events: on HAI, z during test attacks has mean 4.3 and standard deviation 6.7, against the N(11.8, 1) fitted on training attacks (§6, distributions). Each dataset has few distinct test events, and DeepQCD's three seeds differ by up to 0.2 in PD at the same PFA. A lower validation loss does not mean a higher PD here: training scores long geometric transients, while the test asks for the first 25 steps.

Open questions

  1. Is the scalar Gaussian log-likelihood direction z an acceptable input to window_dec_stat on multivariate data, or should the benchmark use another fitted model (for example, the sum of per-sensor Gaussian log-likelihood ratios)?
  2. The Section 5 notebooks use the LSTM with cross-entropy, used here; the Section 6 code uses a two-layer GRU with batch normalization and mean-squared error. Should the real-data runs use the Section 6 network?
  3. Which construction should be primary for real data: the authors' blocks, which match their IoT evaluation, or native events, which avoid splicing but give fewer experiments?
  4. Should τ₁ = 1000 and K = 25 be kept for every dataset, or set per dataset from its time scale (one step is a second on HAI, a minute on SMD and 1/32 s on Daphnet)?
