Summary

  • Question. When a market maker has to offload a position (at once, gradually, passively, by short-gamma hedging) or pulls its quotes, the order flow switches from two-sided noise to a one-sided or self-reinforcing regime for a while and then switches back: a transient change. Can a firm that sees only the anonymous public book and trades detect it quickly, and tell it apart from informed trading after news?
  • Data. A synthetic E-mini-style futures market on the S&P 500, started at the last close (__CLOSE__ on __DATE__, FRED) with its recent volatility (__SIGMA__ a day over 60 days), and walked forward __NDAYS__ trading days. Six market makers, three quoting tight and three wide, each with its own stress policy; every stress episode is labelled.

  • Caveat. All of this is in a simulator whose rules we wrote. It shows that the problem is well posed and which unwind styles are detectable in principle; it does not show that the same edge exists in a real market. §6 lists how to test that on real data.

1Why this is a change-detection problem

A market maker earns the spread and carries inventory risk. When a position grows past its limits, for example after taking the other side of a large client options trade off the exchange, it has to lay that risk off in the lit market. How it does so decides what the change looks like on the tape:

Stress policies, the change each produces, and the closest case in the DeepQCD paper.

The hard part is not seeing pressure but attributing it. After news, informed traders also send one-sided orders, and the book and trades look much the same. The difference shows up later: informed trading moves the price to a new value, while a forced unwind pushes it away from value and the price comes back when the selling stops. So the test streams contain news as a confounder (label 0).

2The synthetic market

One step is one second of a 6.5-hour session; prices are on a 0.25-point tick. The value of the index has a public part Pt, a random walk with the S&P 500's recent volatility, an intraday U-shape and slow volatility clustering, and a private part It, which jumps at news times (about 10 a day, standard deviation 6 points). Market makers quote around a common fair-value estimate Ft = Pt + Gt, where the flow-implied part moves with signed aggressive volume and decays toward It:

Gt+1 = Gt + λ · (buy volume − sell volume)t + η (It − Gt),   λ = __LAMBDA__ points per contract, η = 1/300 per second.

Noise traders send Poisson market orders (about 2 a second, more at the open and close) with a slowly drifting buy/sell imbalance. After news, informed traders buy or sell until the price has caught up with the new value. Each market maker quotes a bid and an ask around Ft − κ q (its inventory q skews its quotes); the tight makers always quote a one-tick market, so the inside spread is one tick, as in E-mini futures. Every second the market orders are matched against the posted quotes, best price first, pro rata at a price.

The six market makers. Stress events arrive about 15 times a day at a random maker; inventory shocks are 150–400 contracts, a random side. A maker's quotes are centred κ·q below fair value when it holds q contracts, so a large inventory shows in its quotes; the scenario switch above divides every κ by 4.

4Detection

The paper's transient protocol (§5.3, notebook cells 19 and 22). Each test stream has one stress event at τ₁ = __TAU1__ s and news at its usual rate; an alarm before τ₁ is a false alarm, an alarm in [τ₁, τ₁ + K) with K = __K__ s is a detection. __NS__ streams per policy and __NS__ news-only streams, at random times of day, none seen in training. Each detector has one threshold, set so that its false-alarm probability over all stress streams is α; a firm watching the tape does not know which policy it faces. The first __WARM__ s of each stream are a warm-up for every detector.

  • DeepQCD: the paper's LSTM(16) → Dense(10) → Dense(1), trained as in the notebooks (Adam, cross-entropy, batch 32, 20 epochs) on 3,200 windows of 3,000 s from the training days, label 1 while any market maker is under stress; the 9 features standardized by unlabelled-normal training data.
  • Window-limited CUSUM: window_dec_stat over the last __WLK__ s, one per stress policy, each on the Gaussian log-likelihood direction of that policy against normal data (fitted on the training days); each is divided by its own 95% quantile under no stress, and the statistic is the largest of the six, so that every policy gets a fair share of the false-alarm budget. It also gets the absolute values of the signed features, because stress can push either way.
  • Flow z-score: |signed volume over the last __FW__ s| in training standard deviations; the usual trader's rule.

5Is a detection worth anything?

A detection is worth something only if the price is still away from value when the alarm comes. For each directional stress (instant, TWAP, skew, lean) that a detector caught at α = 0.05, the table gives how far the stressed flow had pushed the price from value at the alarm, in the direction of the stressed maker's trades. That distance is known only inside the simulator; it is what will come back. It also gives the profit of trading against the stress at the alarm and closing 1 or 5 minutes later, net of a 1-tick cost. The same is given for alarms after news, where fading is the wrong trade. Everything is in ticks of 0.25 points, and over 5 minutes the price also moves about ±24 ticks at random, so the standard errors matter.
