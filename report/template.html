Summary

  • All functions come from the authors' code (Sec. 5.3 notebook and Sec. 6.2 IoT script), ported from TensorFlow to PyTorch. Each port is tested against the original loop.
  • Section 5 reproduced. Our curves match the authors' saved notebook outputs. Bayesian: DeepQCD is within 6% of the Shiryaev optimum in 6 of 7 settings. Minimax: the advantage over CUSUM exists only with the change at τ = 1; with the change at τ = 200, CUSUM is as fast or faster. Transient (Fig. 9): PD 0.87 vs 0.86 at PFA 0.05, as in the paper; the window-limited CUSUM is better for weaker (μ = 0.5) or shorter (K = 10) changes.
  • Transient QCD on real data (six datasets that meet the assumptions of Sec. 5.3; τ₁ = 1000, K = 25; PD at PFA ≤ 0.05, DeepQCD vs window-limited CUSUM): HAI 0.43 vs 0.51, SMD 0.03 vs 0.26, SKAB 0.15 vs 0.15, Occupancy 0.95 vs 1.00. Neither detects freezing of gait.
  • Splicing. The authors' stream construction joins normal and event data recorded at different times. This creates a jump at τ₁ up to 20 times larger than the real onset. The one case where DeepQCD leads (Occupancy without the light sensor, 0.47 vs 0.20) disappears without splicing.
  • Open questions are in §8.

4Transient change (paper Fig. 9)

The change is present only on [τ₁, τ₂): f₀ = N(0, 1) before and after, f₁ = N(μ, 1) during. Every experiment ends in exactly one of three ways: a false alarm (Γ < τ₁), a detection (τ₁ ≤ Γ < τ₂) or a miss, so PD + PMD + PFA = 1, and the goal is the largest PD subject to PFA ≤ α (Eq. 11). The network is trained on changes lasting about 500 steps on average and tested on changes lasting K = 25 steps that start at τ₁ = 1000. The benchmark is the window-limited CUSUM. It knows f₀, f₁ and K.

5Datasets

The transient model is f₀ → f₁ → f₀, so the real data must provide labelled events that start and end. The paper's test (τ₁ = 1000, K = 25) adds requirements of its own. A dataset qualifies when:

  1. C1 its labels mark intervals that start and end, after which the data returns to normal (not a persistent change, a single instant, or an onset alone);
  2. C2 most events last at least K = 25 steps, because the test scores the first K post-change samples;
  3. C3 at least five test events are preceded by τ₁ − 1 + K = 1024 or more normal steps, so the paper's τ₁ = 1000 can be tested on real, unspliced data;
  4. C4 there are enough events for disjoint training and test sets (at least 5 each, lasting ≥ K).

Table 3 applies these conditions to every labelled dataset in the project. HAI and Daphnet meet all four. SMD fails C2 because most of its anomalies last under 25 minutes, so it is tested on the 60 test events that last at least K. Occupancy and SKAB fail C3. Occupancy has a loader in the authors' own code; it is used with all five sensors and again without the light sensor, which makes arrivals much harder to see. Before an arrival the office is empty overnight for about 14 hours, under 1,000 minutes, and SKAB runs last 20 minutes. At τ₁ = 1000 only the blocks construction of §6 can test these two, using the few long normal stretches available (one weekend for Occupancy) or, for SKAB, chained stretches from different runs. τ₁ = 200 is therefore reported as well. Change-point, persistent-fault and single-instant datasets fail C1 and are not used.

Eligibility of the labelled datasets. Each dataset name links to the GitHub repository it is downloaded from. Events are counted after the project's train/test split. "≥ K" counts test events lasting at least 25 steps; "≥ 1024 before" counts test events that also have at least 1024 normal steps before them.

Data preparation

Table 4 lists how each dataset is read and split (realdata.py). The same steps then apply to all of them:

  1. Each recording is cut at its labels into normal stretches and events. An event runs from its first to its last labelled step, and the normal data after it belongs to the next normal stretch. An event is used only if enough normal steps precede it (the minimum is in Table 4).
  2. The normal pool is every normal stretch of the training split, plus any event-free recordings. The event pool is every training event, from its onset.
  3. Every observation is standardized by the mean and standard deviation of the normal pool, as in the IoT code (do_normalization).
  4. The scalar z for the window-limited CUSUM is fitted on the two training pools (§6).
  5. Streams are then formed as in §6: training streams by the §5.3 generator, test streams as blocks or native.

6Real-data protocol

Training uses the notebook's generator unchanged (N cell 7). It draws τ₁ ~ geo(0.001) and τ₂ − τ₁ ~ geo(0.002), then fills t < τ₁ with a contiguous block of real normal training data, [τ₁, τ₂) with a real training event from its labelled onset, and t ≥ τ₂ with another normal block. This is the authors' IoT construction (random_sample) applied to the transient shape. A real event cannot last longer than it did, so τ₂ is moved to the event's end when the geometric draw runs past it. All inputs are standardized by the normal training data (do_normalization). The network (all sensors, as the IoT code feeds all 115 features) and its training are those of Table 1.

Testing follows N cells 19 and 22 with τ₁ = 1000 and K = 25, in the two ways drawn in the third and fourth rows of the timeline in §4. Blocks, the authors' construction (random_sample_eva), runs 10,000 experiments; each has a normal test block of τ₁ − 1 + K steps (the first K start the CUSUM window) followed by the first K steps of a real test event. Native uses no splicing: each test event is preceded by its own recorded normal stretch, giving one experiment per event that has at least 1024 normal steps before it.

Addition not in the original code. window_dec_stat accumulates x − μ/2: the log-likelihood ratio of N(μ, 1) against N(0, 1), divided by μ. It needs a scalar with that model. Each multivariate observation is therefore mapped to z = w′(x − m₀)/√(w′C₀w) with w = C₀⁻¹(m₁ − m₀), where m₀ and C₀ are fitted on normal training data and m₁ on training events. Under Gaussian models with a common covariance, the log-likelihood ratio of x is exactly a linear function of z, z is N(0, 1) before the change, and μ is the mean of z over training events. window_dec_stat is then called unchanged. As a control, a second DeepQCD is trained on z alone, so the two detectors can also be compared on identical information.

Blocks versus native

The two constructions can disagree sharply; Occupancy without the light sensor is the clearest case. The authors' block construction puts a normal block recorded at one time in front of an event recorded at another, so the data can jump at τ₁ even when the real event begins gently. Table 6 measures this jump. It is the distance between the mean of the 10 steps before τ₁ and the first 5 steps after it, in standardized units, for the real onset and for the spliced one. The last column is the same distance between two random normal blocks: a splice with no change at all.
