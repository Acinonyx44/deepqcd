Summary

We reproduced DeepQCD (Kurt, Zheng, Yılmaz, Wang, J. Franklin Inst. 2024) from the authors' own code, tested its transient form on real sensor data as asked, and then looked for problems in finance where it beats the detectors practitioners use. In every study the network and its training are the paper's (LSTM(16) → Dense(10) → Dense(1), cross-entropy, 20 epochs; original_qcd.py). The test is the paper's transient protocol on native streams: an alarm before the event is a false alarm, a first alarm during it is a detection, and each detector's threshold maximizes PD subject to PFA ≤ α (Eq. 11).

What works. DeepQCD beats the window-limited CUSUM, a learned per-step logistic model and industry rules when three things hold together:

  • thousands of labelled events;
  • an event that shows as a combination of cues, read against the entity's own history;
  • a strict false-alarm level.

Crypto price runs, card fraud and laundering meet all three. What does not:

  • Few events. On real sensor data and Polymarket news there are only 7–155 labelled events, and DeepQCD ties or loses.
  • One statistic. When a single number carries the event (a price rise, a status, signed flow), the CUSUM is as good and needs no training.
  • No trading edge from detection alone. Most of what an alarm detects is already in the price.

Every study at a glance. "Best other" is the strongest of the window-limited CUSUM, a logistic model on the current step (learned, no memory) and the rules of the trade, at the same false-alarm level.

1The problem, the detectors, and how data becomes a stream

Model. A stream x[1], x[2], … is drawn from f₀ until τ₁, from f₁ on [τ₁, τ₂), and from f₀ again: a transient change (paper §5.3). The label bits y[n] are 1 exactly on [τ₁, τ₂). A detector turns x[n] into a statistic d[n] and alarms at Γ, the first n with d[n] ≥ h. Each experiment ends in one of three ways, so PFA + PD + PMD = 1:

  • False alarm: Γ < τ₁.
  • Detection: τ₁ ≤ Γ < τ₂.
  • Miss: no alarm before τ₂.

Sweeping h traces a PD–PFA curve. We report PD at PFA ≤ 0.01, 0.05 and 0.1.

From records to x[n] and y[n]. Every study turns raw records into the same objects (Figure 5). Records are split by the thing being watched (a market, an account, a card, a stock) and aggregated on a clock: one step per second, minute, hour, day, month or transaction. Each step is mapped to the features the business already computes (listed for every study in Appendix A), and standardized with the mean and standard deviation of normal training data (the authors' do_normalization). The labels come from a rule applied in hindsight, so every event has a known τ₁ and τ₂. Test streams are native: the real history before the event, then the event, never spliced. They are split from training by time, or by bank, market or client.

Reading the numbers

  • PFA, probability of false alarm. The share of test streams in which the alarm rings before the event starts (before τ₁). Streams without any event count too: an alarm anywhere in them is a false alarm.
  • PD, probability of detection. The share of event streams whose first alarm rings during the event, between τ₁ and τ₂. An alarm before τ₁ is a false alarm. No alarm until τ₂ is a miss.
  • PMD, probability of a miss. No alarm until the event is over. For a stream with an event, PFA + PD + PMD = 1.
  • α, the false-alarm budget. Each detector gets its own threshold: the one with the highest PD whose PFA is at most α (the paper’s Eq. 11). Detectors are compared at the same α, never at the same threshold, because their statistics are on different scales.
  • ADD and FAP. These measure changes that do not end (the Bayesian and minimax settings of §2 and §5). ADD is the average delay from the change to the alarm. FAP is the average time between false alarms when nothing changes. Lower ADD at the same PFA or FAP is better.
  • Reading a PD–PFA curve.
    • Each point is one threshold. To the left the threshold is strict (few false alarms); to the right it is permissive.
    • At any PFA, the higher curve is the better detector. The dashed vertical line marks the α quoted in the text.
    • Curves can fall at the right end: a very low threshold rings before the event starts, which counts as a false alarm instead of a detection.
  • Example. “PD 0.46 at PFA 0.05” in the crypto study means this: with the threshold set so that 5 of every 100 quiet six-minute windows raise a false alarm, the alarm catches 46 of every 100 price runs while they are still running.
  • Seeds and noise. DeepQCD is trained three times from different random starts; the thick line is seed 0 and the thin lines are seeds 1 and 2. With n test events, a PD near p has a standard error of √(p(1 − p)/n): about 0.03 for n = 260 and 0.012 for n = 1,680.

2Reproduction of the paper (synthetic data)

The authors' functions were ported from TensorFlow to PyTorch and each was tested against the original loop. Our curves match the saved notebook outputs.

  • Bayesian setting (§5.1–5.2). DeepQCD is within 6% of the optimal Shiryaev rule in 6 of 7 settings.
  • Minimax setting. Its advantage over CUSUM exists only for a change at τ = 1, where a recurrent network can be extra alert at start-up. For a change at τ = 200 it is never more than 1% faster than CUSUM.
  • Transient change (Fig. 9). PD is 0.87 against the paper's 0.86 at PFA 0.05. The window-limited CUSUM, which knows f₀, f₁ and K, is better for a weaker change (μ = 0.5) or a shorter one (K = 10).

Reading. With unlimited labelled data the network learns the optimal detector; it does not beat it.

5Rare changes: train where changes are common, deploy where they are rare

Credit, laundering and fraud are rare, but labelled examples are easiest to collect where they are common. In the Bayesian setting the posterior's log-odds move by log(ρ_dep/ρ_tr) when the change rate changes. The paper's §5.1 model trained at ρ = 10⁻³ and run at 10⁻⁴ gives:

  • Detection is unchanged. ADD at PFA 0.01 is 22.5 against 22.3 for the optimal rule.
  • The threshold has to be recalibrated, as in the diagram below.

Reading. Train on enriched data, calibrate on it, then shift the prior. Neither step needs labelled data at the rare rate. The result is about twice as conservative as the exact posterior (PFA 0.028 against 0.062 at α = 0.1).

6Prediction markets (Polymarket)

6.1 Does money move before the news?

__T_PMNEWS__

Reading. Only 64 surprise events in 2,083 markets, 34 for training. DeepQCD ties a price rule. Buying YES at any alarm earns less than buying at a random hour, so trading picks up before events but follows the price. Too few events, and no edge.

6.2 Order flow labelled by wallet

Polymarket settles on chain, so every trade carries its wallet. Episodes can be labelled exactly from public data while the detectors see only anonymous flow, the way they would on Kalshi or futures. A metaorder is one wallet working a large order in pieces; it moves the price 2.6¢ on average. Churn is one wallet buying and selling back and forth.

__T_FLOW__

Reading.

  • Churn: win. The signature is busy but balanced and repetitive, a combination the CUSUM and the trade count cannot express.
  • Metaorders: tie. They are mostly one statistic, signed flow. The CUSUM catches the price-moving ones earlier and saves more per metaorder (0.17¢ against 0.04¢).
  • Order clock. One step per order instead of per minute fails for every detector (PD ≤ 0.09).

6.3 Does an alarm make money for a market maker?

__T_MM__

Reading.

  • Quoting against every taker loses money. Over 5 minutes the takers are better informed.
  • Churn is the most toxic flow (−0.37¢ per share against −0.23¢ during metaorders), so the churners are skilled scalpers.
  • Alarms help. Stepping back on them takes P&L to break-even.
  • A "busy" rule does almost as much, because most volume trades in busy minutes.
  • The gap is the value of better metaorder detection. Only a perfect metaorder oracle makes money (+1.86¢ per $100), and the gap to it is what a better metaorder detector would be worth.

7Market makers: a toxic-flow alarm on crypto

A market maker quoting ETH loses when the price runs: whoever trades just before the run knew something. The alarm should ring early in the run so the quotes on that side can be pulled.

__T_TOXIC__

Reading: win.

  • Detection. DeepQCD catches 0.46 of runs against 0.21 for the best rule, and 0.34 against 0.12 at PFA 0.01. The gain is on the smaller 15–20 bps runs (0.43 against at most 0.13) that simple alarms miss.
  • Value. It rings later (median 22 s into a run of about 35 s), but catches so many more that it avoids 2.6–2.7 bps of adverse move per run against 1.6 for the best rule: about 60% more.
  • Data. With 40 days instead of 112 the gain was smaller (0.35 against 0.20).

8Banks

8.1 Laundering in a simulated bank (IBM AMLSim)

__T_AMLSIM__

Reading: win. The network learned a combination: a dormant account (67% of laundering accounts made no transfer in the 120 days before) that suddenly deals with a new counterparty (93% on day one). A dormant-account rule covers part of it. On accounts that were active before their scheme, DeepQCD flags 0.50, against at most 0.12 for any rule or the CUSUM. AMLSim's normal behaviour is simple, so these cues are cleaner than in a real bank.

8.2 Laundering in a realistic bank (IBM AMLworld)

__T_AMLWORLD__

Reading: win at the strict level, tie at the loose one. 87% of laundering transfers are ACH (12% of all transfers), so one yes-or-no rule, "ACH with a new counterparty", reaches 0.64 at PFA 0.05. Being binary, it cannot work below its own false-alarm rate. At PFA 0.01, which is where a bank that must keep alerts down operates, DeepQCD finds 0.41 against 0.27 for the logistic model. We also tried Elliptic++ (real Bitcoin wallets) and dropped it: wallets live a median of one time step and labels never change, so it is classification, not change detection.

8.3 Card fraud and account takeover (IBM TabFormer)

__T_FRAUD__

Reading: win, most where issuers operate. At PFA 0.01, DeepQCD catches 0.54 of bursts against 0.33 for the logistic model with the same features. The gain is largest when fraud starts online (0.76 against 0.60): an online purchase is normal for one card and alarming for another, and only the card's history tells which. Every detector lets one fraudulent transaction through before its alarm (median).

8.4 Credit-card default

__T_CREDIT__

Reading: tie. At PFA 0.2, the usual credit operating point, every detector is at 0.62–0.64, and "months late now" is as good as any. Six monthly steps give memory nothing to work with, and the strongest signal is the current status. Freddie Mac or Fannie Mae loan histories would be the stronger test, but they need a registration.

9Hedge funds: short-squeeze early warning

__T_SQUEEZE__

Reading: tie. A squeeze is mostly one statistic, the price going up, which a short window over returns already sees. Alarms come about 5 days into a 93% rally with about 17% of it left, saving about 13% per rally for DeepQCD and the CUSUM alike. The volume-spike rule catches fewer rallies (0.41) but earlier, and saves the most (17.6% per rally). For short sellers, speed beats coverage.

  • The memory is what pays. The logistic model sees the same features one step at a time and loses the winning races by 0.1–0.3 in PD.
  • Detection is not profit. Polymarket and the squeezes show that an alarm is worth something only if it comes while the move is still ahead, and markets price most of what can be seen.
  • Use it where labels are free. Card issuers (chargebacks), banks (case files), market makers (markouts and price runs), and venues with public wallets. In those settings the network replaces several hand-written rules with one score, at the false-alarm levels those teams use.

11Limits and next steps

  • Synthetic data. TabFormer, AMLworld and AMLSim are synthetic (AMLworld and TabFormer realistic). Real bank and issuer data would be the next test.
  • Thresholds. Thresholds are set on the test streams, as in the paper. In production they would be set on held-out data with the rare-event recipe of §5.
  • Hindsight labels. The crypto run labels come from prices. A real market maker's own markouts would give labels that fit its quotes exactly.
  • Replay model. The Polymarket replay ignores queue position and liquidity rewards.
  • Next. The crypto toxic-flow alarm on more symbols and venues. A metaorder detector trained on Polymarket's wallet labels and run on anonymous venues. Loan-level credit data with long histories.

AFeatures and rules in each study

The features are those each business already computes from its records; nothing is learned before the network. All are standardized with normal training data before they reach DeepQCD, the logistic model and the CUSUM projection z. Counts, volumes and amounts enter as log(1 + ·), and signed flows as sign · log(1 + |·|). The rules use the raw features. “Combined” scales each rule by its 99th percentile on normal training data and takes the maximum, the way a bank runs several scenarios at once.
